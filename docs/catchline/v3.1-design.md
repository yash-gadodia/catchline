# Catchline 3.1 design: body-frame measurement, any angle, 100% on the iPhone

Written 20 Oct 2026 from experiments run that day (scripts and raw outputs listed in the appendix). This replaces the
measurement parts of `on-device-plan.md`; the plan's phases 0-9 are superseded by section 7 here.

The decisions Yash made on 20 Oct are taken as given: the lifter's body is the basis (not the plates), 3.1 is 100%
offline on the phone with no server analysis or fallback, iOS only, designed and timed for the iPhone 16 (older phones run, slower), a soft target of about 2 minutes for a 12 s clip with a stage-by-stage progress screen,
no training or tuning on user data, re-analysis of old lifts deferred, a "please update" screen for 3.0.

## 1. Summary of decisions

1. **The bar is the hands.** The bar position is `bar_from_wrists` (the wrist midpoint, with the far wrist dropped when
   it strays), smoothed and stabilised, in a body frame anchored at the ankles with the lifter's facing direction as +x.
   Plates are an optional booster used on a few setup frames for scale and a second yaw estimate only. They never gate a
   result and are never tracked through the lift. Evidence: on 15 well-tracked side-on owner reps the wrist bar and the
   plate agree to a median 3 cm through the whole pull, and the plate track is lost in 75% of turnovers anyway (section 2).
2. **Scale comes from the body.** px per cm from the standing shoulder-hip-knee-ankle chain (vertical segments lose
   under 3% of their length at 53 deg yaw). The plate, when its outline is held, only cross-checks it. Absolute cm are
   therefore within about 8% of truth per person (limb-length spread); every threshold already carries more margin
   than that, and the cues that matter most are timing and ratios.
3. **Yaw from the body, with honest error bars, once the estimator is fixed.** Camera yaw off side-on from the
   shoulder and hip widths, averaged, clamped to 0-70 deg. The model says plus or minus 3 deg at 20 deg, 7 at 45, 9
   at 53, saturating past 65. On the owner's clips the current estimator under-reads by a median 16 deg and collapses
   to 5-9 deg on the two steepest clips (section 2.5), so fixing and proving it is phase 2's first exit criterion;
   the current pipeline is worse (it silently assumes 0 deg on 10 of 24 clips). Per cue type: timing and vertical cues are valid at any yaw; sagittal joint angles are valid
   to 45 deg with an angle-aware threshold; forward/back distances are reported to 45 deg, flagged "estimate" to
   60 deg, and not judged beyond. At Jer's 53 deg a forward distance is known to within about plus 33 / minus 12%, so
   it can still fire when it is 1.5x the threshold.
4. **No 3D lifting in 3.1.** MotionAGFormer (Apache 2.0) is the only candidate that is licence-clean, accurate and cheap
   enough, but every lifter is root-relative, its depth-axis error is about 2x its lateral error (about 29 mm per joint
   on studio data), and all are trained on Human3.6M whose weights licence is academic-only. What we lose: forward/back
   distances filmed front-on. That stays "film from the side for this cue" (section 3.5).
5. **Movements: clean & jerk as one lift with two segments and one cue; variations by start class.** A rest-then-rise
   rep finder in the body frame replaces "wrists below the knees". Prototype on the public set: hang snatches went from
   0 of 4 clips found to 4 of 4; clean & jerk from the clean only to both parts in 5 of 13 (section 4). Phase
   definitions per start class (floor, hang, blocks, shoulders) and the rules each class allows are in section 4.3.
6. **Speed: pose only the lifter, detect at FP16 on the Neural Engine.** Today's pose pass on an idle M4 is 66 ms per
   frame on an app clip (detect 27, pose 27 for 5.6 people, resize 11). An FP32 Core ML program is barred from the Neural
   Engine, so everything runs on the GPU. Posing one or two people instead of all, and running YOLOX at FP16 on the
   Neural Engine, brings the M4 cost to about 25-30 ms per frame, which puts an iPhone 12 at roughly 40-50 s for a 12 s
   clip with plates and stills included (section 5). The per-device number must be measured on a real iPhone 12.
7. **Offline: the phone decides.** Free lifts are a Keychain counter (3 for life), paid status is the StoreKit 2 /
   RevenueCat cache on the device, the server only records what happened when the phone is online. Models are bundled.
   The account becomes optional. Modal, the Mini worker, the queue, the upload buckets, analyses, traces, coach links and
   the "ready" push are deleted (section 6).
8. **One implementation: TypeScript is the oracle.** The Python pipeline is retired as a reference once the TS port
   (#65) is the place the redesign happens. The macOS Swift harness plus node become the eval pipeline on the Mac
   (pose in 10-20 s per clip there). Python keeps only the scoring scripts and the Core ML export (section 7).

## 2. Measurement core in the body frame (A)

### 2.1 Decision

- **Bar point:** `bar_from_wrists(L_WRI, R_WRI, 0.12 body heights)` as today, then a 3-frame median before the 6 Hz
  low-pass (the one-frame wrist flip that broke turnover timing on 9fb313e4). No forearm extension to the grip: the
  wrist keypoint sits at the hand's base, which is 2-4 cm from the bar axis, a constant offset that cancels in every
  displacement and timing measure we use. A learned bar or hand detector is not needed for 3.1 (section 2.4).
- **Frame:** per rep window, stabilise with the camera path (as today), then express every point relative to the
  ankle midpoint at lift-off (origin) with +x = the facing direction from the toe-heel vector at lift-off, +y up. The
  mid-foot (ankle + 0.4 foot length) is the balance reference for "in front / behind" copy.
- **Scale:** `scale_from_body` (90th percentile of the shoulder-hip + hip-knee + knee-ankle chain over the clip,
  divided by 132 cm) becomes the primary px per cm. The plate (when a refined outline is held on a setup frame and its
  diameter is within 0.75-1.75x the body scale) replaces it only when the lift is within 20 deg of side-on, because
  there the near plate's depth ratio is measurable; off side-on the plate ellipse is both a worse scale and a worse
  angle than the body.
- **Plate fusion (Yash, 20 Oct: use all three signals).** Body and hands are always on. The plate is tracked
  through the whole lift wherever it is confidently held, and a plate frame refines the bar point only when it agrees
  with the hand bar within 6 cm (in the body frame); otherwise that frame is ignored. So the plate can only sharpen a
  result, never break one (floor-patch fits, another lifter's bar, re-locks onto a dark patch are all rejected by the
  agreement test). Confidence is highest when body, hands and plate agree, normal with body and hands, lower at steep
  yaw for forward/back distances.
- **Smoothing and glitches:** keep the zero-phase 6 Hz Butterworth; add the 3-frame median on the wrists before it;
  keep `select_lifter`'s shift gates; a frame whose wrist moves more than 0.25 body heights from the previous smoothed
  bar is interpolated, not used.

- Wrist rise over plate rise through the pull: median 1.00 (p75 1.1, p90 1.2). Vertical velocity correlation 0.7
  median. The hands are the bar through the pull.
- The plate track survives the turnover in only a quarter of the frames and disagrees by 8-15 cm where it does: the
  plate is blurred and often leaves the frame. Every catch measurement today already comes from the wrists.
- The 3-4 cm floor is the sum of pose noise (about 2 px at 1280 tall is 1 cm), the plate's own 0.05-diameter tracking
  grid, and the near plate's perspective (it is closer to the camera than the bar centre, so its px scale differs by

Across all 25 reps (including the 10 where the plate was coarse, not on the bar, or the clip was angled) the pull
medians stay at 3.8 cm vertical and 2.9 cm horizontal, but the p90 blows out to 10-33 cm in the second pull and
turnover: those are plate failures (re-lock on floor clutter, the 2.5x floor patch), not wrist failures. On this set
5 of 25 reps already fell back to the body scale and 2 were "plate not on the lifter's bar".

  first in phase 2: the clip-median includes squat and overhead frames where the shoulders stack; RTMPose swaps left
  and right on the far side so the width collapses; the torso length used to normalise is the hinged (foreshortened)
  one. Until that is proven, no cue decision may rest on the yaw estimate alone, and the forward/back cues at
  40-60 deg must stay "worth checking" at best, as they are today.
- **Scale:** the current pipeline used the body scale anyway on 6 of 24 clips with a rep (plate fit unreliable or
  the plate not on the bar) and a near-plate depth ratio of 0.73-1.05 on the rest; the body prototype's absolute cm are

- **Rep finding (v2 prototype):** agrees with production on 18 of 24 barbell clips; it finds a rep on the three clips
  production cannot (AC99E154 and both cleans), over-splits two squat snatches (E3F099FB, F8523D6B) and misses the

- **Gymnastics:** unchanged in both modes (the body is already the basis there); two of three muscle-ups fail on the

label beyond the eyeball check. The follow-up list is in section 8.

### 2.4 Rejected alternatives

- **Plate as the bar (today's design).** Fails without both plates (Jer), fails on camo and speckled plates (coarse
  fits 25-40% large), re-locks on floor plates (2d294d90), and is lost in 75% of turnovers. It was the right bootstrap
  for scale and angle on side-on clips and remains a booster for exactly that.
- **A learned bar-end or hand detector (YOLOX-nano, ~1k labels).** It would locate the sleeve to about 1-2 cm and work
  for a bar held in a hook grip at any angle. Deferred: the wrist already gives 3 cm through the pull, the plate was
  not the problem the body frame cannot solve (angle is), and it costs 1.5-3 weeks of labelling plus a second Core ML
  model on every frame. Revisit if the 3.1 eval shows hand-based bar error above 5 cm on real users' turnovers.
- **Forearm extension to the grip.** Adds the elbow's noise to the wrist's for a constant offset that cancels anyway.
- **Plate-based scale everywhere.** The near plate's scale needs the depth ratio from its own rise, which needs the
  plate tracked through the pull; off side-on its ellipse is not an angle (the coarse grid returns 0 or 60). The body
  chain is within 3% of its length to 53 deg (section 3.2, table 4).

## 3. Any-angle handling (B)

### 3.1 Decision

- **Yaw estimate:** `off_side_pose` as today (shoulder width over 0.7 torso, hip width over 0.5 torso, each through
  asin, averaged), measured on the frames between lift-off and the second extension (upright or hinged, not the squat
  catch), median over frames, clamped to 0-70 deg. The plate ellipse adds a third estimate only when its outline was
  refined; the median of the available estimates is the yaw. Foot direction (toe-heel vector angle) is recorded as a
  diagnostic but not used: feet turn out 10-30 deg from the sagittal plane and a split jerk breaks it.
- **Error model:** plus or minus 3 deg at 20, 5 at 30, 7 at 45, 9 at 53, 12 at 60; unusable past 65 (table 1 below).
  `ANGLE_ERR_DEG` becomes a function of the estimate, not a constant 10.
- **Per cue type validity:**

| cue type | examples | valid to | how the threshold moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| timing | early_arm_bend, slow_turnover, early_arm_pull, chicken_wing, drive-to-press | 70 deg | unchanged; the elbow lockout test (150 deg) is the one angle inside it: it reads 12 deg more open at 53 deg (table 3), so lockout is detected a frame or two early. Use 150 + 0.25 x yaw as the lockout angle |
| vertical | hips_rise_early, bar_crash, catch depth, bar peak, hips_too_low_at_start, bmu/hspu heights | 70 deg | unchanged (y is yaw-invariant under orthographic projection; perspective adds under 2% at phone distances) |
| sagittal joint angle | incomplete_extension (hip 170), press_out (elbow), knee 65 (squat vs power), dip knee | 45 deg | a bent joint reads more open: 120 reads 142 at 53 deg. Thresholds move by the table-3 amount; beyond 45 deg the catch kind and incomplete extension are reported as "estimate", press-out is not judged |
| forward/back distance | bar_forward_off_hips, jump_forward, forward_dip, bar_forward_overhead, chest_dive, shoulders_behind_bar, no_push_away, hspu_banana | 45 deg as a value; 60 deg as a flag | value is on-screen / cos(yaw); its 68% band is +4/-3% at 30 deg, +14/-8% at 45, +33/-12% at 53, +76/-18% at 60 (table 2). A cue fires at 20-45 deg when the on-screen value corrected at (yaw - err) clears the threshold (today's `on_screen` rule, with the new err), at 45-60 deg only when it clears 1.5x, never past 60 |
| lateral (left/right) | none today; foot width, elbow flare | front-on only | not in 3.1 |

- **Confidence copy:** "filmed about N deg off side-on" stays; the "clear" level needs yaw under 30 and two reps.

Facts that decide it:

- Every lifter outputs root-relative joints: whole-body forward/back travel (jump forward, bar vs mid-foot) is not in
  the output at all. Only VideoPose3D has a trajectory head, and it is non-commercial.
- Per-axis error on H36M for the MixSTE/D3DP family: x 14 mm, y 15 mm, z 29 mm (AAAI 2025, "Learning Knowledge from
  Textual Descriptions for 3D Human Pose Estimation", table 6). Depth is the axis a front-on camera needs, at twice the
  error, before any in-the-wild penalty. The faults we flag are 20-80 mm.
- Trained on Human3.6M: 4 fixed cameras at chest height, walking and sitting at 50 Hz; a 15 deg camera rotation already
  costs 68 mm MPJPE (CanonPose). A phone on the floor filming a snatch is out of distribution.
- Human3.6M's EULA is academic-only; every public checkpoint is trained on it. The code is Apache, the weights are a
  grey area no repository addresses.
- Cost is fine: MotionAGFormer-B would be 1-3 s per 12 s clip on an iPhone 12 after a first-of-its-kind Core ML
  conversion.

Recommendation: do not ship 3D lifting in 3.1. Without it, a front-on clip gets timing, vertical and joint-angle cues
and a plain "film from the side to judge forward/back" for the rest. If a later version wants front-on forward/back,
MotionAGFormer-B is the candidate, used as a classifier ("clearly forward" vs not), never as centimetres, after an
eval against dual-phone footage (section 8).

Sources: licence files via raw.githubusercontent.com for each repo; MotionAGFormer arXiv 2310.16288; MotionBERT arXiv
2210.06551; D3DP arXiv 2303.11579; CanonPose arXiv 2011.14679; RootNet arXiv 1907.11346; VideoPose3D arXiv 1811.11742;
Human3.6M EULA vision.imar.ro/human3.6m/eula.php; Apple Vision docs developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vndetecthumanbodypose3drequest.

### 3.5 What one 2D camera cannot do (plain statement)

- Measure forward/back distance front-on (within 20 deg of front). Not with 2D, not usefully with 3D lifting.
- Measure forward/back distance past 60 deg off side-on to better than a factor of two.
- Tell a 5 cm jump forward from zero at 45 deg (the band is plus or minus 1 cm of noise times 1.4 foreshortening,
  with the yaw error on top: 8 cm is the honest threshold there, as today).
- Separate a hang start from a block start without the bar's own rest signature (section 4.2).
- Absolute centimetres better than about 8% without a known object; the plate gives 3-5% side-on only.

## 4. Movements (C)

### 4.1 Decision

- **One rep finder, body frame, rest-then-rise.** A lift is: the bar (wrists) at rest for 0.25 s or more, then a rise
  of at least 0.8 torso lengths within 2.5 s, then received, which is a hold of 0.3 s or more either overhead (above the
  head stand-in) or at the shoulders (rack mask). The start class is read from the resting bar height against the
  body: `floor` (below the knee), `hang` (knee to hip, and the bar was 0.3 torso higher in the 3 s before: the lifter
  stood up with it), `blocks` (knee to hip, no earlier higher position, bar static 1 s or more), `shoulders` (rack).
  Power vs squat stays the catch-depth test (knee angle, yaw-corrected per table 3).
- **Clean & jerk = one lift, two segments, one cue.** A `clean` segment followed by a `jerk` segment that starts in the
  rack within 8 s is one lift record `clean_and_jerk` with `segments: [clean, jerk]`, each with its own phases, key
  frames and measurements. The cue is chosen across both segments by severity with one extra gate: a clean fault that
  explains a jerk fault (a forward rack catch explaining a forward dip) wins. The other segment's top fault appears
  as a note. A jerk that is missed after a made clean reports "clean made, jerk missed" as the outcome.
- **Detection:** the movement picker keeps the user's choice as a hint; `detect.py`'s read decides between the family
  members (snatch / clean / jerk / clean & jerk) from the receive position, as today. New: a user-tagged "clean" that
  shows a jerk afterwards becomes clean & jerk; a user-tagged "snatch" that starts at the hang is a hang snatch.
- **Gymnastics unchanged** (kipping pull-up, bar muscle-up, kipping HSPU): they already use the body and the bar line.

`exp_c_reps.py` runs the production finders (`find_reps`, `find_rack_reps`, `find_jerk_reps`) and the rest-then-rise
prototype over the tracked lifter's cached pose for the 91 barbell clips, against the uploader's rep count in the
title.

Reading:

- Hang snatches: the production finder needs the wrists below the knees and found none in 3 of 4 clips; the prototype
  finds snatches in all 4 (start class `hip` or `hip+stand`). It over-counts one 10-rep clip as 8 and one as 3 (two
  reps merged in the rise test): the merge rule from `find_reps` (same rep until the bar is back at rest) is needed.
- Clean & jerk: production stops at the clean (the rack ends the rep). The prototype found clean then jerk in 5 of 13
  clips and clean only in 6; 2 front-on clips read the clean as a snatch (the rack mask needs the elbows forward,
  invisible front-on: use the bar-at-shoulder-height test alone when yaw is over 60).
- Plain snatches: the prototype under-performs the production finder (13 vs 20 exact) because it splits a squat
  snatch's stand-up and re-dip into two rests. The design is therefore the production finder's overhead/rack holds as
  the receive test, with rest-then-rise replacing only the "below the knees" start test. That union is what section 4.1
  describes; it was not run as one function in this experiment.
- Jerks: both finders are weak on the public jerk clips (front-on, 2-4 lifters in frame, bar racked from a rack). The
  jerk fixes of #59 and #62 are in the production code used here; the prototype's `shoulders` start class works (9 of
  13 right kind) but the overhead hold test fails on the front-on clips where the head stand-in sits above the wrists.
  Front-on jerks are out of scope for cues anyway (forward/back is the whole fault list); they still need reps found
  for the "no cue, filmed front-on" result.
- Start classes seen across all prototype reps: floor 72, hip 47, shoulders 50, knee-to-hip 5. "Blocks" never
  appeared: the public set has no block clips, and neither do the owner's. Blocks will be labelled by the user
  (variation picker) in 3.1 and the start-class read only confirms or warns.

### 4.3 Phases and rules per start class

| start class | phases | rules that apply | rules that do not |
|---|---|---|---|
| floor (snatch, clean) | setup, first_pull, transition, second_pull, turnover, catch, recovery (as today) | all | |
| hang | setup (standing with the bar), hinge (bar lowered to the start height), second_pull, turnover, catch, recovery | early_arm_bend (from the lowest bar point), incomplete_extension, bar_forward_off_hips, jump_forward / back, slow_turnover, bar_crash, catch depth; new: `hang_no_rebend` (knee angle at the bottom of the hinge, catalogue clean_hang_no_rebend), `hang_shoulders_behind` | hips_rise_early, hips_too_low_at_start, shoulders_behind_bar_at_start (no first pull) |
| blocks | setup (bar resting), pull_from_blocks (one pull to the second extension), turnover, catch, recovery | as hang, minus the two hang rules | as hang |
| shoulders (jerk) | setup, dip, drive, turnover, catch, recovery (as today) | forward_dip, press_out, chest_dive, bar_forward_overhead | |
| clean & jerk | clean segment phases, then rack_hold, then jerk segment phases | both lists, cross-gated | |

Key-frame and still logic follows the phases. The `hinge` phase of a hang start is the countermovement: its end is the
lowest bar point before the rise, which is also where the pull measurements begin (`lo`).

### 4.4 Rejected alternatives

- Two cues for a clean & jerk. Rejected: the product is one cue a lift; two cues double the "why" reading and split
  the retest tracking. The second segment's fault is a note.
- A separate "hang" and "block" movement in the picker with their own detectors. Rejected: the same lift, the same
  rules minus the first pull; one finder with a start class keeps the code and the eval in one place.
- Learned rep segmentation (a small temporal model over the pose). Rejected for 3.1: no labelled data we may train on,
  and the rest-then-rise signal is physical.

## 5. Speed on an iPhone 12 (D)

### 5.1 Measurements

Release harness (`modules/pose-coreml/harness`, `release/3.1` at 497e8e7, `--no-camera`), Mac Mini M4 10-core GPU,
idle (load 2.6-3.0), FP32 Core ML, compute units `.all`, 1280 px tall inference:

| clip | frames | people / frame | resize ms | detect ms (infer) | pose ms (infer) | total ms / frame | wall s | peak RSS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 21B27BAF original (3840x2160) | 153 | 5.6 | 38 | 28 (25) | 27 (24) | 94 | 14.3 | 510 MB |
| 21B27BAF app clip (608x1080) | 153 | 5.6 | 11 | 27 (24) | 27 (24) | 66 | 10.1 | 269 MB |
| E0DF8887 app clip | 190 | 7.3 | 11 | 27 (24) | 34 (31) | 73 | 14.0 | 291 MB |
| EDF10BB7 app clip (BMU) | 389 | 2.1 | 12 | 27 (24) | 10 (9) | 50 | 19.1 | 269 MB |

- Pose cost is linear in people: 4.3 ms per person per frame (24 ms at 5.6, 31 at 7.3, 9 at 2.1). Detect is a fixed
  24 ms. Decode is under 1 ms (hardware). The same run on the laptop under a load of 58 from other agents gave 565 ms
  per frame, which is why the Mini numbers are used.
- Everything after pose, measured as the Python pipeline's non-pose time on the Mini CPU across the 79 public clips
  with timings: median 29 ms per frame (p90 57), i.e. a median 17 s per clip (p90 40 s) for a median 624 frames. That
  is decode for the rep windows, stabilisation (KLT), plates (find, refine, track) and stills. Plates are most of it on
  the clips that have them.
- An FP32-typed Core ML program never runs on the Neural Engine: "Only the NE is barred as a compute unit when a model
  is typed with float 32 precision" (coremltools, Typed execution guide). So today's models run on the GPU, and the
  A14's Neural Engine (11 TOPS) is idle. FP16 RTMPose moved keypoints 13 px p95 in the earlier export test, so RTMPose
  stays FP32 in 3.1; YOLOX only produces boxes that RTMPose pads by 1.25x, so FP16 detection is safe.

### 5.3 What must be measured on a real phone

1. Pose pass ms per frame on an iPhone 12 and on Yash's phone, app clip, with the compute-unit report
   (`MLComputePlan`, iOS 17.4+) to confirm where each model runs.
2. YOLOX FP16 on the ANE: ms per frame and box agreement with FP32 (IoU over the eval set; anything over 0.9 on the
   lifter box is fine because RTMPose pads the crop).
3. Thermal: three 12 s clips back to back; does the third slow down (the A14 throttles under sustained GPU load)?
4. The resize path: CPU vImage vs a Metal shader; 11 ms on an M4 could be 40 ms on an A14.

### 5.4 Rejected alternatives

- Lower pose input (RTMPose-s, 192x256 at a smaller backbone): saves about 40% of pose time but 2-3 mm keypoint loss
  on the lifter; posing fewer people saves more with no loss. Keep in reserve.
- Detect every Nth frame as the primary saving: the earlier test (14-10) found detector-every-2nd-frame unsafe for
  plate scale; with plates gone it is safe for the lifter, but FP16 on the ANE is the bigger, simpler win. Keep as the
  fallback if the ANE path underperforms.
- 720 px inference height instead of 1280: a 2.8x pixel saving, but the lifter is often a third of frame height and
  "bar_lost" lifts are already the small lifters. Not for 3.1.

### 7.2 Where the redesign happens: TypeScript, with the Swift harness as the pose source

Decision: redesign directly in `src/analysis/device/` (TS) and `modules/pose-coreml` (Swift). Python is not changed
first and then ported.

Why: the goal is no Mac Mini and no server, which means no Python in production. The TS port already exists at exact
parity (#65), so it is the same code; changing phases and rules in Python and porting again is double work and a
second parity suite for every change. The Swift harness produces a clip's pose and trace on the Mac in 10-20 s
(faster than the Python pipeline, which upscales and runs ONNX on the CPU), so the eval loop on the Mac is harness
plus `node` over traces. The experiments in this document were run in Python because the Python debug tooling
(contact sheets, overlay videos, signal plots) exists and the TS has none; that tooling is the one thing to replace:
a small node script that renders the same contact sheet from a trace plus the analysis JSON (no pipeline internals).

Cost: numerical work without numpy (already done for the port: `num.ts`, `filter.ts`) and slower experimentation
for whoever prefers Python. Mitigation: the trace format is the contract, so a Python notebook can still read traces
for one-off analysis; it just cannot be the reference.

### 7.3 Phases with exit criteria

Each phase is a PR series on `release/3.1` (native) or `catchline` (logic), merged only on its exit criterion, so no
phase is redone.

**Phase 1: eval loop on the Mac (3-4 days).** Harness with the camera stage, `node` runner over traces, contact-sheet
renderer, the 36 owner app clips and the 77 public barbell clips as the fixed sets, a scoreboard script (rep recall,
cue agreement, timing deltas). Exit: one command runs the whole set from clips to a scoreboard, under 30 min on the
Mac, with 3.0's cues as the baseline column.

**Phase 2: body-frame core (4-5 days, TS).** `bar_from_wrists` + 3-frame median, body frame, body scale primary,
plate booster on setup frames only, yaw from the body with the error model, per-cue validity table (section 3.1).
Exit: on the side-on owner set every 3.0 cue is reproduced or changed with a written reason per lift (no silent
drift); on the 45-degree public clips the no-cue rate drops from 59% (3.0 baseline on the full set) with zero cues
fired from a forward distance past 60 deg.

**Phase 3: movements (4-5 days, TS).** Rest-then-rise start + hold receive, start classes, hang and block phases,
clean & jerk segments and cross-gating, yaw-aware lockout and knee thresholds. Exit: rep recall at least 90% on the
labelled public barbell clips (today 32 of 43 snatch reps, 0 of 10 hang snatch reps), clean & jerk found as two
segments in at least 10 of 13 public clips, no new false reps on the owner set.

**Phase 4: speed (4-6 days, Swift).** Lifter-only pose, YOLOX FP16 on the ANE, plates tracked through the lift where
confidently held, stage timing in the dev route. Exit: measured on an iPhone 16 at or under about 2 minutes for a 12 s,
5-person clip, three clips back to back, with the progress screen showing each stage, and the lifter's keypoints within 1 px median of the all-people FP32 run on the eval set.

**Phase 5: offline app and server (3-4 days).** Keychain free counter, RevenueCat cache gate, lazy account,
`sync_lift`, bundled models, privacy copy, "update" screen for 3.0. Exit: airplane-mode end to end on a fresh install:
three free lifts, the fourth paywalled, a sandbox purchase restored from cache, then online sync shows the lifts and
cue memory on the dashboard once.

**Phase 6: ship 3.1 (1-2 days + review).** Phased release; the 3.0 update screen on day 0.

**Phase 7: server deletes (1 day, day 14).** Section 6.4.

Total: about 4 weeks of build before review, versus the 4-6 weeks in the old plan plus 1.5-3 weeks of plate detector

  to build; labelling about 2 hours.

### 8.3 Metrics and the acceptance bar for 3.1

| what | metric | bar |
|---|---|---|
| reps | recall and precision against labels, per start class | at least 90% recall, 95% precision on the owner set and the labelled public set |
| bar position | median error vs the clicked sleeve centre, cm, side-on and 45-55 deg | side-on under 4 cm; angled under 6 cm through the pull |
| yaw | absolute error vs the dual-phone placement and the 15-degree bins | median under 8 deg to 55 deg |
| timing cues | delta vs the side-on reference for the same lift, frames | within 2 frames at 50 deg |
| vertical cues | delta vs side-on reference, cm | within 3 cm at 50 deg |
| forward/back cues | fire / no-fire agreement with the side-on reference at 45-55 deg | no cue that the side-on view contradicts; at least half of the side-on cues reproduced |
| speed | iPhone 16, 12 s clip, 5+ people | at or under about 2 minutes, three in a row (soft target) |
| no-cue rate | share of analysable lifts with a cue, public 45-degree group | up from the 3.0 baseline, reported per reason |
| regression | owner set, cue per lift vs 3.0 | every change listed with a reason |

The scoreboard runs in CI on the Mac runner against the fixed sets (clips never leave the Mac; the scoreboard JSON is
what is committed).

## 10. Risks

- **The x4 iPhone 12 scaling is a guess.** If the phone is x6, the budget is 65-75 s and the fallbacks (detect every
  3rd frame, RTMPose-s) go in. Measure in phase 4's first day.
- **FP16 YOLOX on the ANE misses small lifters.** Detection recall on lifters at a third of frame height must be
  checked against FP32; if it drops, detect at FP32 on the GPU every 2nd frame instead.
- **The body yaw estimator under-reads steep angles on real clips** (section 2.5: 5-9 deg where the eye says
  50-65). The synthetic error bars in section 3.2 assume the widths are measured on upright frames with correct
  left/right labels; the real measurement is not doing that yet. This is the first thing phase 2 must fix and prove
  (hypotheses listed in 2.5), with the dual-phone session as the check; the fallback is to keep today's angle policy
  (forward/back cues at 20-70 deg are "worth checking" at most) and move the "value" limit from 45 to 35 deg.
- **Rest-then-rise over-counts squat lifts.** Seen in the prototype; the merge rule is specified but not yet proven in
  one function.
- **Front-on jerks produce no cue.** By design; the copy must say why.
- **Bundled models and App Review.** A 150 MB jump is unusual but not a rejection reason; the privacy labels change
  ("video is not collected").
- **Deleting `analyses` loses lifts for users who never open 3.1.** The one-time move runs on first 3.1 launch; the
  server copies stay until day 14 after 3.1 is live, then go. Users who return later see cue-only history.
- **Two code bases during phases 2-5.** `catchline` (3.0, OTA-able) and `release/3.1` diverge for about a month;
  only fixes that 3.0 users need go to `catchline`.
