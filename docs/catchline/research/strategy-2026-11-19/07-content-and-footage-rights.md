## 1. Footage rights

**Ownership and policy**
- **IWF:** IWF documents state it is "sole and exclusive owner" of audio-visual material and media rights for its events. Sportfive is its exclusive rights partner to 2028 ([sportbusiness](https://sportbusiness.com/news/sportfive-retains-iwf-international-rights-until-2028)).
- **Clip rights are sold:** the 2026 tender for the LA 2028 qualifiers lists "social media clip rights" as an asset ([IWF tender PDF](https://iwf.sport/wp-content/uploads/downloads/2026/03/IWF-Tender_TV-Production_2028-OG-Qualifiers.pdf)). I found no IWF fan-reuse policy.
- **Olympics:** the IOC and NBC routinely issue takedowns, and Instagram briefly blocked Elaine Thompson-Herah for posting her own race ([LBC](https://lbc.co.uk/tech/c0bc3ea5533548e99c624a7076d3080a), [WCNC](https://wcnc.com/article/news/verify/olympic-video-social-media-posts-can-be-blocked-by-ioc-nbc/536-025f37c6-d16a-46f4-953a-4b14e149b302)). Avoid Olympic footage entirely.

**Platform rules**
- **Instagram:** accounts that mostly repost are dropped from recommendations until 30 days after their last unoriginal post. A watermark, border or caption credit does not make a post original ([PetaPixel, Apr 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)). Whether a bar-path overlay plus voiceover counts as original is unconfirmed.
- **TikTok:** content is ineligible for the For You feed if "unoriginal or reproduced" without "new or creative changes" ([Community Guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/integrity-authenticity/)).
- **YouTube:** not checked.

**Fair use / fair dealing** (statutes and cases recalled from memory, except the Singapore sources linked)
- **US:** four-factor test (17 USC 107). Analysis helps, but promoting an app is a commercial purpose and weighs against (Warhol v Goldsmith, 2023).
- **UK:** fair dealing for criticism, review or quotation is narrower. A UK court found sports clips shared through an app were not fair dealing ([Conventus](https://conventuslaw.com/report/singapore-english-high-court-considers-the-defence/)).
- **Singapore:** open-ended fair use under ss 190-191 of the Copyright Act 2021. MinLaw's factsheet describes removing the fifth factor ([MinLaw annex](https://www.mlaw.gov.sg/files/news/press-releases/2021/03/ANNEX-Copyright-Factsheet.pdf)). I did not read the enacted text.

**Right of publicity:** not researched. From memory, US state laws target use of a likeness to advertise, and Singapore relies on passing off (false endorsement). Never put an athlete's name or face in the same frame as "download".

**In practice:** I found no documented strikes against weightlifting analysis accounts. The Olympic pattern is post removal via the platform.

**Sourcing, ranked safest first**
1. Own lifts.
2. App users' lifts with written consent (add an in-app opt-in on export).
3. Athletes' own training-hall posts, with permission. Use Collab or Remix so the original is credited natively.
4. Federation-licensed or Creative Commons footage. Ask Singapore Weightlifting; British Weightlifting publishes an event video policy ([BWL PDF](https://britishweightlifting.org/resources/2025-bwl-event-video-and-photography-policy-261125153631.pdf), not read).
5. Public-domain archive footage: rare, and status is hard to prove.
6. IWF broadcast clips: highest risk, organic only, short, heavily analysed.

**Permission DM (draft):**
> hey [name], big fan of ur lifting. i built a free iPhone app that draws bar path + peak bar speed on lift videos. can i run ur [lift, date] clip through it and post the breakdown, tagging u as collab? will send it to u first, and happy to take it down anytime. no worries if not

## 4. First 30 days

Confirmed:
- Reels can run up to 3 minutes since January 2025 ([Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-reels/)).
- Instagram tests each reel on a small non-follower audience and widens the best performers ([Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/helping-creators-of-all-sizes-break-through)).
- Instagram's official per-account guidance on frequency and length sits in the in-app Best Practices hub ([Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/best-practices-for-creators)). The public page gives no numbers, so read it in the app once the account is professional.
- Collabs, Remix and Reposts are the sanctioned ways to share others' work (PetaPixel, above).

Unconfirmed (blogs or memory):
- A 5-hashtag cap on Instagram, and keywords in captions mattering more than hashtags.
- Other apps' watermarks suppressing reach; TikTok's guidelines do not mention them. Export clean from Catchline anyway.
- 15 to 30 seconds performing best.
- Replying to comments in the first hour.
- TikTok's official guidance on frequency and length: none found.

## 6. Viewers to installs

- **Custom product pages:** up to 70, each with a unique URL, its own screenshots, search keywords and a deep link (iOS 18+). Each reports impressions, downloads and conversion in App Analytics ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/)). Build one per format family (bar path, speed, CrossFit) and use its URL as the bio link, so installs are attributable per theme. Apple's wording names "social media ads or promotions", not bios.
- **Comment-to-DM on Instagram:** allowed through Meta's Private Replies: one private reply per comment within 7 days, more only after the person responds. ManyChat is a Meta Business Partner ([Planoly](https://www.planoly.com/blog/is-comment-to-dm-allowed-on-instagram), [ManyChat](https://manychat.com/blog/instagram-dm-automation-rules/); vendor sources, Meta's docs not read). Forum posts report comment bans after high-volume days, so start with one keyword.
- **TikTok:** bio-link eligibility and any comment-to-DM equivalent are unconfirmed.
- **On-video call to action:** no data found on wording. My suggestion is the last 2 seconds showing "Made with Catchline. Free on the App Store", plus a small persistent corner mark so reposts carry the name.
