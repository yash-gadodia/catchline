## tasteless
1. notificationAsync(Success) on Copy plus the checkmark SymbolView bounce ('animationSpec bounce — the entire celebration budget'). This is a tri-tap celebration haptic AND the app's only bouncing element, placed on the single most emotionally loaded action — copying a sentence to send to the person you just fought with. The design register says never celebratory; the design-system artifact already killed Success and the other two artifacts smuggled it back. Kill both: impactAsync(Medium) once, label crossfades to 'Copied', no symbol animation. 2. 'Almost there.' at t=25s — a promise the app cannot verify against unbounded LLM latency; if the response takes 60s this line is a lie the user watched. Dishonest microcopy performs reassurance. Let 'A considered answer, not a fast one.' stand alone. 3. 'A considered answer, not a fast one.' itself is borderline — the interface praising its own virtue mid-wait. Acceptable once; would test 'Still reading.' as the quieter alternative. 4. Copy-unlock scale tick (1.0→1.02, the app's sole sub-0.85 damping) — a wiggle on a state change with no causal user gesture; the haptics track's own causality rule condemns exactly this in haptic form. Color transition alone carries the unlock. 5. Strikethrough of the AI's superseded phrase for a 300ms beat while the user edits — 'honesty as a visual pattern' is performative here: it puts machine residue inside the user's sentence at the precise moment they are claiming ownership of it. The Flighty move belongs to superseded DATA, not to a sentence being adopted. Cut. 6. 'ｘ Discard' using a fullwidth ideographic x — a typographic hack pretending to be a glyph; use xmark SymbolView or plain text 'Discard'. 7. Bridge sentence demoting from InstrumentSerif-Italic to Hanken 17pt while editing — the sentence turns into UI chrome exactly when the user makes it theirs; edit in the italic serif (RN TextInput renders it fine). 8. The 0.13-bounce 'breath' on the bridge card container — reintroduces the bounce the same document system banned, on the reveal of the most serious content in the app; dampingRatio 1, the Light haptic is the breath. Notably ABSENT tastelessness: the glass posture is exemplary (zero in-app GlassView, system-drawn glass only, expo-glass-effect not even installed), the no-bridge stillness preset is the best single piece of design in the three artifacts, and zero-sound-as-policy with the silent-switch-bypass argument is genuinely first-rate reasoning.

## most_likely_wrong
The Copy interaction stack — gated-on-edit AND rendered disabled-quiet AND unlocked only by editing or a small text link AND then requiring a hidden 450ms press-and-hold. Every layer has a defensible essay behind it, but compounded they mean: an upset person reaches the one sentence the app exists to produce and meets a button that looks broken and doesn't respond to a tap. Disabled-looking primary actions get abandoned; hidden hold gestures are the least discoverable interaction on iOS; and this user has the least patience of any user in any app. The cheap way to find out: a 30-minute unmoderated test before any code — a clickable Figma (or the existing result-screen mock) given to five people with one instruction, 'send this sentence to your partner,' and two measurements: did they find a way to copy within 15 seconds, and did anyone tap-and-give-up on the held button. If two of five stall, the answer is the unstacked version in change 2. (Factual runner-up, even cheaper to check: the 'iOS will ask twice' permission-count claim — one dev build, one device, ten minutes.)
